# R5 Nimitz 2004 — records schedule / disposition request

## Goal
Identify the disposition authority that actually governed USS Princeton (CG-59) operational electronic records on 14 November 2004, rather than inferring record fate from later schedules or from a generic “No Records Located” FOIA result.

## Suggested recipient
NARA Records Control Schedules team (`RCS_Team@nara.gov`) for schedule identification, with follow-up to the Department of the Navy records officer or FOIA component if required.

## Request text

I am researching records-management provenance for USS Princeton (CG-59) operational electronic records from 14 November 2004. I am not asking NARA to evaluate any UAP claim. I am asking which disposition authority applied to specific Navy record classes on that date.

Could you please help identify the NARA-approved schedule/item or SSIC that governed the following USS Princeton / AEGIS operational records in November 2004:

1. AN/SPY-1 / AEGIS target-track or radar-return records;
2. Combat Information Center logs;
3. electronic-warfare / ESM operational records;
4. air-defense-control-net audio or communications;
5. exercise or significant-event reconstruction records generated by the ship/combat system.

Historical schedule N1-NU-89-005 (Operations and Readiness Records) is relevant because its text includes SSIC 3560 “Combat Information Center Logs” with a three-year disposition and SSIC 3610–3613 electronic-warfare/ESM/ECM/ECCM record families. NARA’s current notice indicates that most remaining N1-NU-89-005 items were later superseded by DAA-NU-2015-0003, approved in 2018.

I do **not** want to assume that AN/SPY-1 raw radar/track data were filed under SSIC 3560 or 361x. The purpose of this request is to determine the actual 2004 governing classification and disposition authority.

If available, please provide:

- the controlling schedule/item and SSIC for each named data class in November 2004;
- whether that authority required temporary or permanent retention and the retention period/event trigger;
- whether shipboard tactical/radar data could have been treated as nonrecord/working data under a separate authority;
- the office/component that would hold any transfer, destruction, or disposition documentation;
- any known transfer/disposition records specifically involving USS Princeton CG-59 for the relevant November 2004 exercise period.

This request is motivated by a Navy FY2023 FOIA request that sought Princeton radar returns, E-2C radar returns, air-defense audio, sonar data and official reports for 10–16 November 2004 and was closed “Full Denial – No Records Located.” I am trying to determine the ordinary records-management pathway that could explain that outcome, not infer concealment from it.

Thank you.

## Scientific gate
Success = exact 2004 schedule/item mapping and, ideally, a transfer/destruction/disposition record for the named data class.

Not success = generic later retention policy, another witness account, or interpreting “No Records Located” as proof of destruction or concealment.
